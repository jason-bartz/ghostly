Meetings
- Ghostly can now transcribe a call live, in a floating panel you can read while the meeting is still happening. It never joins the call and nothing leaves your Mac.
- Both sides of the conversation are captured: your microphone, and the call's audio through a macOS process tap. Your lines and theirs are labelled separately, and you can click any name to rename that speaker.
- Where were we? summarises what you missed, with what is being discussed right now, the decisions made, anything asked of you, and one line you could say to rejoin the conversation.
- Ending a meeting writes a summary automatically.
- Pause stops capturing without ending the meeting, so stepping away does not end the transcript.
- If someone says your name, the panel tells you.

Finding meetings again
- A new Meetings tab keeps every transcript and summary, searchable across titles, transcripts and tags.
- Filter by date or tag, group by day or by app, rename a meeting, and export one meeting or all of them as Markdown, plain text or JSON.
- Meeting transcripts are deleted automatically after the retention period you choose.

Starting a meeting
- Start or end a meeting from the menu bar, from the Meetings tab, or with a shortcut you assign.
- Ghostly can notice when a call starts by checking whether a conferencing app is actually using your microphone, rather than merely running. It never starts recording silently: you either confirm, or a countdown runs that you can cancel.
- Auto-connect is off by default, and you can exclude meetings whose name contains words like therapy or 1:1.

Requirements and privacy
- Capturing the other side of a call needs macOS 14.2 or later. Below that, Ghostly transcribes your side only and says so.
- No screen recording permission is needed.
- Meeting transcripts are never written to the log file or included in a diagnostics bundle.

Fixes
- App names are now matched by their real bundle identifier. Per-app styles and categories were silently failing for most apps, including Slack, Teams and Messages, because the app was comparing against a display name.
- The sliding highlight in tab switchers was pushing labels to the right, and the date picker in Notes expanded the toolbar instead of floating above it. Both were caused by the same styling conflict.
- Every screen now uses the same width, title and toolbar layout. Notes was wider than everything else, and most screens had no title at all.
- Dictation started during a meeting no longer fails with a misleading model not loaded error.
