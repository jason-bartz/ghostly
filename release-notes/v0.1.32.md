The meeting transcript reads like a conversation
- Each stretch of speech is now its own block, sized to what was said. A three-word aside stays a small one; a long explanation gets a paragraph. Nothing is stamped with a name.
- Speaker labels are gone. "You" and "Participant" were only ever a guess at which microphone the audio arrived on, and it was wrong often: two people sharing a laptop were one speaker, and your own voice coming back off the call was a second one. A label that unreliable is worse than none.
- Naming, merging, adding and reassigning speakers went with them. They existed to repair a guess that should not have been made, and there is nothing left to correct. Correcting the words themselves still works, on any line, in the Meetings tab.
- Summaries still know who said what. That is the one place the distinction earned its keep, so it is kept there and only there.

Lines stopped appearing twice
- The two halves of a meeting's capture — your microphone, and the audio from the call — were keeping separate time. They start at different moments, because the microphone is already open while the system tap takes a few seconds to come up, and each one was counting from its own first frame. Measured on a real call, they were twenty seconds apart.
- Anything both halves picked up was therefore written down twice, worded slightly differently each time, and nothing could tell that the two copies were the same sentence. A reopened transcript could also come back with its two sides interleaved in the wrong order, and summaries were built from that shuffled version.
- Both halves now share one clock, and it corrects itself if either falls behind. Echo between them is suppressed in both directions rather than only one.

Transcription is sharper
- Speech is split where you actually stop talking, rather than at every half-second pause. Those pauses fall inside sentences, so the old boundaries produced fragments like "So." and "Exotic car, so" — and short fragments are also the least accurate thing to hand a transcription model, because there is no surrounding context to decode against.
- The AI cleanup pass now reads a whole passage at a time instead of one fragment. It can punctuate across a break, finish a clause with the words that actually followed it, and spell a name the same way twice.

Meetings name themselves
- A new meeting is called "Google Meet meeting - 08/12/26" instead of "Untitled meeting".
- Calls held in a browser tab are recognised by the service they are on — Meet, Teams, Slack, Zoom, Webex, Discord and others — rather than by the browser. A Meet call in Dia used to be recorded as a meeting in "Dia", which told you nothing about it a week later.
- A browser holding the microphone with no call open no longer offers to start a meeting. Voice notes and dictation boxes on ordinary web pages were setting it off.

The meeting panel
- The title bar matches the rest of Ghostly instead of being a purple band across the top, and every part of it drags, including over the logo. The logo used to be a dead spot.
- The window's corners are genuinely round now, shadow included. They were being drawn round on a square window, so the shadow traced the square and gave it away.
- Copy sits beside Export when a meeting ends, for a transcript that is going straight into a message rather than into a file.
- The offer to finish your notes is no longer a card wedged between the notepad and the buttons. It is the Enhance button you already had, which simply becomes prominent — so the end of a meeting is two tidy rows instead of five buttons stacked on each other, whether the notepad is open or folded away.
- You can drag across the transcript to select and copy it without opening an editor.
